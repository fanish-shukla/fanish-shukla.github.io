I'm an Enterprise and Solution Architect based in Zürich, and this page is less about where I've worked and more about what actually keeps me interested in this field after 20+ years in it.

## What Draws Me to Enterprise Architecture

Enterprise Architecture, done well, is one of the few disciplines that sits at the real intersection of business strategy and working technology. I'm drawn to the moments where a messy, ambiguous business problem gets translated into a model that both a CFO and a backend engineer can look at and agree on. That's the craft: not producing diagrams for their own sake, but building shared understanding that survives contact with a real system in production.

ArchiMate specifically has become a language I think in, not just a notation I apply. Business, Application, and Technology layers mapping cleanly onto each other is deeply satisfying in a way that's hard to explain to people outside this field — but if you've ever watched a stakeholder finally *see* how their process depends on a system three layers down, you know exactly what I mean.

## Where My Curiosity Goes Next

I've spent recent years pulled toward a few specific areas:

- **Governed cloud-native transformation** — not "lift and shift," but genuinely rethinking platform expectations for Azure/AKS environments so applications are designed for resilience from day one, not patched into it later.
- **Integration architecture as a discipline, not an afterthought** — moving organisations away from brittle point-to-point dependencies toward event-driven, API-first patterns that can actually evolve.
- **Observability as an architectural concern**, not just an operations one — designing systems so that health, performance, and failure points are visible by design.
- **Architecture in regulated, safety-critical environments** — aviation and financial services both demand a kind of rigor that keeps the work honest; there's no room for a diagram that quietly doesn't match reality.
- **How AI agents change architecture governance** — I've been thinking a lot about what it means to make architectural decisions machine-readable and enforceable, not just documented, now that a growing share of code is written by agents rather than humans. It's part of why I write here.
